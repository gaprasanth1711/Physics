Explore the Physics — hydraulic lift

Principle

Pascal’s law: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished to every part of the fluid. A small force on a small piston supports a large force on a large piston.

Working, step by step

1. Force F1 on area A1 creates pressure P = F1 / A1. 2. The same pressure P acts under area A2, giving F2 = P × A2. 3. The fluid volume pushed down (A1 × d1) equals the volume lifting the load (A2 × d2), so d2 = d1 × A1 / A2 — the load moves less than your hand.

Equations

P = F / AF1/A1 = F2/A2A1·d1 = A2·d2

Real world

Car lifts, hydraulic jacks, forklifts and aircraft landing gear all trade distance for force this way.

Question

With A2/A1 = 25, how far must the small piston travel to raise the car 25 cm, and how many 20-cm presses is that?

d2 = d1 × A1/A2, so d1 = 25 cm × 25 = 625 cm = 6.25 m. At 20 cm per press that is 32 presses (31.25, rounded up).

Pedal force vs wheel clamping force

Explore the Physics — hydraulic brakes

Principle

The pedal lever multiplies your foot force, the master cylinder turns it into fluid pressure, and that pressure pushes the larger wheel pistons. Small master area plus large wheel area gives the clamping force.

Working, step by step

1. Pedal lever: Fm = Fp × R. 2. Master cylinder: P = Fm / Am. 3. Each wheel piston: Fw = P × Aw. 4. Pads clamp the disc; friction there (not the hydraulics) makes the braking torque.

Equations

Fm = Fp × RP = Fm / AmFw = P × Aw

Force vs torque — read carefully

Hydraulics only creates the clamping force. Braking torque needs pad–disc friction (τ ≈ F × μ × r) and stopping the vehicle needs the full vehicle dynamics. The slowing wheel here is illustrative, not a stopping-distance calculation.

Real world

Car disc/drum brakes, bicycle hydraulic brakes and clutch actuators all use a small master piston driving larger slave pistons.

Question

Your foot pushes 150 N with a 4:1 pedal onto a 5 cm² master cylinder. Two 30 cm² wheel pistons share the pressure. What is each clamp force — and what did you trade away to get it?

Fm = 600 N, P = 1.2 MPa, Fw = 3.6 kN per wheel. You traded pedal travel: the master piston moves much farther than each wheel piston (volume conservation again).
