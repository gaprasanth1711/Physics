Top 10 Most Important CBSE Class 12 Physics Derivations

Compiled by Prasanth Ganesan — derivations directly account for 15–20 marks in CBSE Class 12 Board examinations. Master these 10 core proofs with scoring tips.

Derivation #1 • Electric Charges and Fields (Ch 1)

Electric Field Intensity on Axial and Equatorial Line of an Electric Dipole

3–5 Marks
$$E_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2p}{r^3},\quad E_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p}{r^3}$$
  • Draw labeled dipole length 2a and distance r from center.
  • Resolve E1 and E2 vector directions explicitly.
  • Apply short-dipole condition r >> a; verify E(axial) = 2 * E(equatorial).

Derivation #2 • Electrostatic Potential & Capacitance (Ch 2)

Capacitance of a Parallel Plate Capacitor (With & Without Dielectric Slab)

3 Marks
$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - t(1 - 1/K)}$$
  • Start from field E0 = sigma / epsilon0 in air gap and E = E0 / K inside dielectric.
  • Write potential V = E0(d - t) + (E0/K)t and substitute sigma = Q/A.
Derivation #3 • Current Electricity (Ch 3)

Relation Between Drift Velocity and Electric Current

2–3 Marks
$$I = n e A v_d,\quad v_d = \frac{e E \tau}{m}$$
  • Derive electron acceleration a = -eE/m and average over relaxation time tau.
  • Compute total charge crossing cross-section A in interval dt.

Derivation #5 • Moving Charges and Magnetism (Ch 4)

Force Between Two Parallel Current-Carrying Conductors & Definition of 1 Ampere

3 Marks
$$\frac{F}{L} = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}$$
  • Like currents attract, unlike currents repel.
  • Always state the official SI definition of 1 Ampere at the conclusion.

Impedance and Resonance in a Series LCR Circuit (Phasor Method)

Refraction Through a Prism & Angle of Minimum Deviation

3 Marks
$$\mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + D_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)}$$

Derivation #10 • Atoms (Ch 12)

Bohr's Quantization Condition & Radius of nth Orbit of Hydrogen Atom

3 Marks
$$r_n = \frac{\varepsilon_0 h^2 n^2}{\pi m Z e^2} \propto n^2$$
  • Equate electrostatic attraction to centripetal force mv^2/r.
  • Substitute mvr = nh/(2*pi) and verify rn proportional to n^2.

4 Golden Rules for Board Exam Derivations

  1. Intro sentence first: Define every symbol cleanly before writing step 1.
  2. Pencil-labeled diagrams: Accurate labeled ray/vector diagrams carry dedicated marks.
  3. Box your final formula: Include its standard SI unit next to the boxed equation.
  4. State special cases: Include boundary conditions like r ≫ a or θ = 0°, 90°.
